**中文**

11.3. 列记忆，10 个生活中真实的瞬间

1. 总是在质问自己。为什么？一直在问为什么？
为什么我要做这些，为什么我在这里。
质问、怀疑一切。我的感觉是真实的吗？
我到底喜欢什么？给出答案又被一一划掉。
我喜欢游戏？有时候也不想打。
喜欢游戏又因为什么呢？
我喜欢读书、写东西？
但也很久没做了。
但是一段时间不做了的，就不能称之为喜欢的吗？
似乎总在追求一个我可以永远做、一直做的，才可称之为喜欢。
似乎一直做但做不出"名堂"的，就什么也不是。
100% or nothing
想到最后，经常开始直接否定自己的一切。

**English**

Nov 3. Listing memories: ten real moments from my life

1. I'm always interrogating myself. Why? I keep asking why.
Why am I doing this? Why am I here?
I question and doubt everything. Are my feelings even real?
What do I actually like? I give an answer, then cross it out, one after another.
Do I like games? Sometimes I don't feel like playing.
And if I like games, why?
Do I like reading, writing?
But I haven't done those in a long time either.
But if I stop doing something for a while, does that mean I can't call it something I like?
It seems I'm always looking for something I could do forever, without stopping. Only that would count as liking it.
It seems that if I keep doing something but never make anything "of it," then it's nothing.
100% or nothing.
By the end of thinking like this, I often just start denying everything about myself.

2. 申请期间，一次因为一直想不出能通过的主题，
过年的时候一直在焦虑、一直在想，
结果拿给老师又被毙了。
当晚睡在床上越想越崩溃，又开始怀疑自己。
爬起来一边流泪一边在纸上疯狂写，
责骂自己，写了很多宣泄的话，真心觉得自己是个废物，
对不起父母，对不起曾经对未来抱有期待的自己，
不明白为何我成了这样。
可能边哭边写了一个小时，后面又一直胡思乱想，
然后开始做焦虑抑郁自测表，做了好几种。
感觉自己失控了，希望知道我到底怎么了。当时我觉得医院会给我一个置信度高的答案，
认为医院开的药可以让我变回"可控"，
于是开始看医院的预约。
当时大概天早上 4、5 点了，
我就靠在床边的木衣柜上，一边看最近的可预约的医生，一边眼睛红肿发热。

当时打算等爸妈早上去打球了，我就出门自己去。
奇妙的是，在我边哭边自伤（言语上）时，
还比较恐慌；
通过几次测试、预约医生，
认为自己大概是有病的时候，
反而平静了。
不过我爸 6 点多进来发现了我。
我最后没有自己去医院，
思前想后之后，跟他们说了，希望他们带我去医院。

**English**

2. During application season, there was a time when I couldn't come up with a topic that would get approved.
All through the New Year holiday I was anxious, thinking about it nonstop,
and when I finally showed it to my teacher, it was rejected again.
That night, lying in bed, the more I thought, the more I fell apart, and I started doubting myself again.
I got up and, crying, wrote frantically on paper,
scolding myself, pouring everything out. I truly felt I was a piece of garbage,
that I had failed my parents, and failed the version of me who once had hopes for the future.
I didn't understand how I had become like this.
I probably cried and wrote for an hour, and then my thoughts kept spiraling.
Then I started taking anxiety and depression self-tests, several of them.
I felt I had lost control. I wanted to know what was wrong with me. At the time I believed a hospital would give me a high-confidence answer,
that medicine from a hospital could make me "controllable" again.
So I started looking at hospital appointments.
It was probably four or five in the morning by then.
I leaned against the wooden wardrobe beside my bed, looking at the soonest available doctors, my eyes red, swollen and hot.

I planned to wait until my parents went out to play ball in the morning, and then go by myself.
The strange thing is, while I was crying and hurting myself (with words),
I was fairly panicked;
but after taking the tests and booking a doctor,
once I believed I was probably ill,
I became calm.
But my dad came in a little after six and found me.
In the end I didn't go to the hospital alone.
After thinking it over, I told them, and asked them to take me.

3. 我一直认为我想要有变化的人生，
不喜欢规矩，不喜欢一成不变的生活。
我一直认为我在意自由，
包括我之前对物质生活的追求，也是因为觉得这样我更有自己选择的自由和底气。
但我对未知的恐惧是巨大的。
陌生的环境，比较困难繁琐的前置，我畏惧。
我憧憬定居国外、做数字游民，
又觉得太多不可知、不可控的东西，
很麻烦、很焦虑，于是 (?) 不想做，觉得做不到。
我想要的自由、变革的生活，
我觉得太累了。
难道我对自由和变化根本没这么欣赏？
事实上，我还挺喜欢高中上学的日子的。

**English**

3. I always thought I wanted a life full of change.
I don't like rules, I don't like a life that never changes.
I always thought I cared about freedom.
Even my past pursuit of a comfortable material life was because I thought it would give me the freedom and the confidence to choose for myself.
But my fear of the unknown is enormous.
Unfamiliar places, and all the difficult, tedious groundwork that has to come first — they scare me.
I dream of settling abroad, of being a digital nomad,
but then there are too many unknown, uncontrollable things.
It feels like so much trouble, so much anxiety, so I don't want to do it, and feel I can't.
The free, ever-changing life I say I want —
I think it's too exhausting.
Could it be that I don't actually value freedom and change that much?
In fact, I quite liked my days in high school.

**English**

4. I always thought, or told myself, that my life was like the boy in "Lamenting Zhongyong" (a prodigy who grew up to be ordinary).
I seemed to mature early,
and I mistook being early for being naturally gifted.
Maybe I'm not unintelligent, but I made far too much of it.
I once truly, naively believed I was different.
I thought mediocrity = failure, that mediocrity is a sin,
that an ordinary life is a meaningless life,
a life wasted.
But back then I couldn't say what a meaningful life was, either.
I naively thought it meant going down in history,
being remembered (by people other than family and friends).

5. I'm drawn to certain phrases and titles:
*The Naive and the Sentimental Novelist*
*The World as Will and Representation*
*Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*
*Klingsor's Last Summer*, the brain in a vat
But I don't know why.

**English**

6. I've always felt my life has been going downhill since the college entrance exam,
because of my major, architecture and urban planning.
At first it was actually fine, not this painful.
But later I found the work, to me, bureaucratic and absurd,
and the effort completely out of proportion to the reward.
The prospects, the whole industry, basically finished.
So I started to suffer, to doubt,
feeling I had lost both my dreams and my bread.

7. Before college, my life seemed more "eventful":
untamed, carefree, like a character in a novel,
different from the people around me.
The one in color in a black-and-white crowd,
the clear face among blurred ones.
So I liked "remembering the old days."
But since college, I often forget what even happened,
everything seems to be in a brain fog.
I've faded too.

**English**

8. In high school I loved to play, to study, to read.
Like a secret agent, I hid from my parents' inspections,
playing games, reading novels, watching films and British and American shows,
devouring them, knowing every one by heart.
I read while walking, while eating, in class.
I finished three or more books a week.
I played ball, I ran, I studied, I listened to rock.
I did so many things, and all of them stayed in my memory.
My mind, my memory, was clear.
Now everything is as if I've become nearsighted.
I'm often in a dazed, hollow state.
A book I read two months ago has left almost no trace in my head.

9. 小时候我讨厌我妈，反抗她，
认为她霸道太过、控制太过。
有时冷血的想法（我自己）都让我觉得反人类。
我报复她，语言和行动上。
认为她是暴政，我是正当的反抗。
现在我当然也会烦她，
但更多的是愧疚。
我一直觉得对不起她。
为什么我不能像她期望的那样？
我觉得她付出很多培养我，
我对不起她的投资。
换个人更好，我让她丢脸。
我不知道为什么我会这么想。
之前我甚至认为，反正我很多也是无所谓的，
我也没有一个我很期望的生活的样子（我不知有没有），
不如就按照她期望的那样生活。
但好像也做不到。我也不知她期望的是什么样的，
但我觉得她永远不会满意。

**English**

9. As a child I hated my mom, and I fought her.
I thought she was too domineering, too controlling.
Sometimes my own cold-blooded thoughts struck even me as inhuman.
I got back at her, in words and in actions.
I thought she was a tyranny and I was the rightful rebellion.
Now, of course, she still annoys me sometimes,
but mostly I feel guilty.
I've always felt I've let her down.
Why can't I be what she hoped for?
I feel she gave so much to raise me,
and I failed her investment.
Someone else would have been better. I make her lose face.
I don't know why I think this way.
Before, I even thought: I don't care about most things anyway,
and I don't have a picture of a life I really want (I don't know if I do),
so I might as well live the way she hopes.
But it seems I can't do that either. I don't even know what she hopes for,
but I feel she will never be satisfied.
