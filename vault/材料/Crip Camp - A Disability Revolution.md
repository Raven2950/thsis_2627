> [!note] Update 1 笔记（从 Notion 导入）
> Type:
> Documentary film / social impact project / disability rights resource
> Focus:
> The history of Camp Jened and the disability rights movement in the United States.
> Main idea:
> The documentary follows disabled teenagers and young people who attended Camp Jened in the 1970s. The camp became an important space where disabled people experienced community, friendship, freedom, and inclusion. Some of the people connected to the camp later became involved in disability rights activism.
> What the film explores:
> - Isolation
> - Institutionalisation
> - Disability discrimination
> - Community
> - Friendship
> - Disabled identity
> - Activism
> - Civil disobedience
> - Political participation
> - Collective action
> - Representation
> - Disability justice
> Why it is relevant:
> This film provides an important reminder that disability should not be understood only as a medical or technical issue.
> Disability is also connected to:
> - Social attitudes
> - Physical and digital barriers
> - Political rights
> - Community
> - Visibility
> - Identity
> - Representation
> - Access to public life
> Important idea:
> “Nothing about us, without us.”
> This means that disabled people should be involved in decisions, designs, policies, and representations that affect their lives.
> Potential connection to my thesis:
> This is relevant to how I approach user research. Disabled people should not be treated only as research subjects who provide information for a designer’s project. They should be treated as people with lived expertise and as potential collaborators in defining the problem.
> It also suggests that I should be careful about:
> - Designing based on stereotypes
> - Presenting disabled people only as vulnerable
> - Framing disability only as a problem to be solved
> - Assuming technology is always the answer
> - Treating accessibility as a business opportunity without considering community needs
> What I learned:
> A practical product can still be connected to larger questions of identity, participation, rights, and representation.
> Questions:
> - Who gets to define the problem?
> - Who benefits from the product?
> - Who has decision-making power?
> - Is the project responding to a community-identified need?
> - How can users challenge or reject my assumptions?
> - How can I represent participants respectfully?
> - Is the product helping users participate, or asking users to adapt to society?
> - Does the design support independence without isolating users?
> Possible thesis connection:
> This could influence the ethics and research process of my project rather than provide a specific product idea.
> It encourages me to:
> - Work with disability organisations
> - Include disabled people early
> - Treat lived experience as expertise
> - Use co-design or participatory research
> - Share findings back with participants
> - Avoid making claims about a whole community based on a few interviews
> Personal reflection:
> Crip Camp expanded my understanding of accessibility from a design problem to a social and cultural issue. It reminds me that a product should not only make an individual task easier; it should also be considered in relation to participation, dignity, identity, and power.
