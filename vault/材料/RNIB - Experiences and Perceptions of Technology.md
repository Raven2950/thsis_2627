> Link：[Voice of the Customer: Experiences and Perceptions of Technology](https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/research-and-data/reports-and-insight/voice-of-the-customer-bpsps-experiences-and-perceptions-of-technology/)
> Type:
> Research report based on surveys and focus groups
>
> Focus:
> Technology experiences of blind and partially sighted people.
>
> Why it is relevant:
> This is a direct example of research based on disabled people’s lived experience. It discusses how technology affects daily independence, accessing information, travel, and social connection.
>
> Key findings to investigate:
> - Technology can support independence and confidence.
> - Cost and lack of accessibility remain barriers.
> - Some users have to teach themselves how to use technology.
> - People may worry about being left behind as technology changes.
> - AI can be promising but also raises concerns about privacy and trust.
> - Users want more involvement in product development.
>
> Possible thesis connection:
> This report shows that “technology for disabled people” is not automatically useful. Adoption, cost, training, confidence, privacy, and emotional responses to failure all matter.
>
> Questions:
> - What happens when an assistive technology fails?
> - How much support is needed after a product is launched?
> - How can a product be designed with users instead of only tested on users?

## 我得到了什么
Blind and partially sighted people feel fairly confident in using connected devices such as laptops, tablets, and smartphones but are having to self-teach and highlight limited opportunities for formal training.
Cost and inaccessibility remain primary barriers to adopting new technology, with a desire for inclusive design and involvement in product development.
