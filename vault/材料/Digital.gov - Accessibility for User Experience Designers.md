> Link：[Accessibility for User Experience Designers](https://digital.gov/guides/accessibility-for-teams/ux-design)
> Type:
> UX guidance
>
> Focus:
> How accessibility should be included in UX research and design.
>
> Why it is relevant:
> This is particularly useful for my thesis because I want the project to be UX-led and based on user research.
>
> Key points:
> - Users may have visual, hearing, motor, cognitive, or temporary limitations.
> - Assistive technology changes how people interact with a product.
> - A researcher should not assume that their own experience represents the experience of disabled users.
> - Research participants should reflect the diversity of actual users.
>
> Questions:
> - How can I recruit participants with different needs?
> - How should I adapt interview or testing sessions?
> - How can I avoid designing based on assumptions?
> - How should I document accessibility requirements during research?
