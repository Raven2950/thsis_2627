s# Starting Over

![From "what I should make" to "what I actually like"](thinking-path.png)

## Why I started over

Before this cycle, my topic was disability and accessibility, especially alt text. I picked it partly because I had access to people I could interview, and partly because it felt practical, like something that would make a good portfolio piece for finding a job.

But while researching it, I found that I'm actually not that interested in it. Then I suddenly realized that a lot of the things I made before were things I felt I *should* make: things that might help me find a job, or things that are practical. I'm not sure I was really interested in them. For a lot of things, my attitude has been kind of "whatever." I don't hate them, but I don't really like them either, so a lot of what I made was also stuff I don't hate and don't like.

Now I want to know what I actually like. I want to dig deeper into myself and find a medium to express what I discover through that introspection. So I decided to throw out what I had and start over. This cycle's explorations were about that.

## Exploration 1: Self-excavation

**Question:** If I set aside what I *should* make, what am I actually drawn to?

**What I did:** I drew five mind maps by hand in my notebook:
- **What I like**: a wide net of films, books, games, layouts, and feelings.
- **Why I love Cyberpunk 2077**: its aesthetics, its worldbuilding, its absurd small details, and its sense of freedom.
- **Why I love Disco Elysium**: its art style, its quotable lines, how the world comes in fragments, and its decadent, absurd feeling.
- **Feelings I like**: sinking, freedom, rebellion, the basement, drinking, scars, Dostoevsky, Siberia.
- **Me**: a page that turned into a list of my own contradictions.![[notebook-p41-EN.jpg]]![[notebook-p39-EN.jpg]]![[notebook-p37-EN.jpg]]![[notebook-p35-EN.jpg]]
![[notebook-p33-EN.jpg]]


**What I found:**
- **Five threads kept coming back:** the fallen, a divided self, the world after ideals fail, absurdity and the search for meaning, and the body and its scars.
- **One core tension:** on the "Me" page I wrote that I only want to live a life that *means* something. On the same page I also wrote that I'm losing my drive and can't express what I feel. I wrote that I love the feeling of writing, poetry, film, aesthetics and music, but I can't say what that feeling actually is. That gap between wanting meaning and not being able to reach or express it feels like the heart of what I want to make.
- **A question I asked myself:** "Do I actually like the washed-up loser vibe much more than elegant old-money style?" Looking at everything together, the answer is yes. What I'm drawn to is art that takes failure seriously and gives it dignity.

## Exploration 2: Dissecting my references

**Question:** I know Disco Elysium and Cyberpunk 2077 attract me, but which part exactly?

**What I did:** I broke both games down layer by layer: visuals, UI, writing, worldbuilding, and mechanics. I started watching the documentary about the making of Disco Elysium, and I collected articles, interviews, and papers about both games and about cyberpunk as a genre. I also gathered other works with a similar temperament: novels, films, games, and philosophy. In total it's a library of about 80 works and sources, sorted by the five threads.

**What I found:** It isn't their worlds that draw me in. It's a temperament they share:
- the main character is already broken before the story starts;
- the world comes after a revolution or rebellion has failed;
- there's more than one voice in your head (the skills in Disco Elysium, Johnny Silverhand in Cyberpunk);
- body and mind are slowly used up;
- the lore comes in fragments you piece together yourself;
- there are moments of grace in the middle of decay.

The difference: Disco Elysium turns inward (the mind is the world), and Cyberpunk 2077 turns outward (the city and the body are the world).

## What changed

I'm choosing games as my new medium. It's a medium I'm familiar with, it lets me express things both visually and through text, and I'm also interested in writing, so I want to write a story to express what I want to say. Since we submit in May, I want to make a complete game: story-driven, in the spirit of Disco Elysium, but much smaller.

But even though the world of Cyberpunk 2077 attracts me, and I might want to express some deeper or philosophical thoughts, I think that if I really wrote a world like Cyberpunk 2077 or Disco Elysium, it would probably end up too ungrounded, because of my time and my ability. I also don't think it would feel genuine enough.

So even though I haven't decided what story to tell, I'll probably write a storyline I personally feel more connected to. I study these games to understand what draws me to them, so I can learn how they create that feeling. Many keywords from my brainstorm won't be directly related to my story. They describe a mood, a kind of temperament.

**In short: the form and texture come from my references. The content comes from my own life.**
