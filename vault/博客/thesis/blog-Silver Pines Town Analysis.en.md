## Building the atmosphere in the opening
The game opens on a black screen with text in the middle. The voices sound like murmuring, sleep-talk, questions.
Then a bell cuts in, and the black screen turns into an animation in a cinematic aspect ratio: a man resting his head on a desk wakes up and gets to his feet.
The atmosphere in this opening is built really well, including the way the lighting works with the scene. (I think the black in the foreground matters a lot, this half-lit feeling. At this point the player knows very little about the game, so the lighting is very immersive.) And at this point you don't know who the main character is. The first time the phone booth appears, the phone rings, and the person on the other end tells the main character who he is and gives you the first key character, so the player roughly understands the main quest.![[20261106195254_1.jpg]]![[20261106195448_1.jpg]]

## How the UI supports it
It's mostly an immersive UI with very little information on screen.
The style of the map UI is great. It really feels like a detective's. It roughly tells the player where they are in the game while keeping the experience as immersive as possible.

## Interacting with special objects
The radio and television carry a lot of the background exposition and the mood here, and I feel they also carry part of a hidden storyline?
I think the interaction with the radio in the opening scene is really interesting. You can turn the dial directly, and on certain frequencies someone talks and you get information.![[20261106195532_1.jpg]]![[20261106195805_1.jpg]]

I also think the first appearance of the phone booth is clever. When you open the door, the first thing you hear is the phone ringing, and only then do you see the phone booth. Using the phone booth to save is pretty new too (at least I haven't played a game that does it). But the whole game has no fast travel, so you have to run everywhere yourself, and you can't save anywhere except at a phone booth. Also, the inventory often runs out of slots, so you have to go back and forth to the storage. That feels inconvenient. But I think the designers did it on purpose.
You can also dial some special numbers at the phone booth to move the story forward, which I think is pretty interesting.![[20261106195813_1.jpg]]![[20261106200214_1.jpg]]

For controls, there's some climbable terrain, but some places don't give you a hint. There was one spot I didn't realize I could climb until I looked at a guide. Maybe leaving out the hint is on purpose, to make it more of a surprise?![[20261106200919_1.jpg]]
