## Previously: I changed my topic

My thesis used to be about accessibility and alt text. I picked it because I had people to interview and because it felt practical for finding a job. While researching it, I realized I wasn't really interested in it, and that a lot of what I've made before was what I felt I *should* make, not what I actually like. So I threw it out and started over (see [Starting Over](#/blog/blog-starting-over)).

In the last two explorations I dug into what I'm drawn to, and took apart the two games I love most, Disco Elysium and Cyberpunk 2077. I decided on my medium: **a complete, story-driven game, in the spirit of Disco Elysium but much smaller.** My conclusion was: *the form and texture come from my references; the content comes from my own life.*

That conclusion raised two questions, and this update is about them:

1. If the content comes from my own life, **where in my life is it?**
2. If the form comes from Disco Elysium, **what exactly is that form made of?**

## Exploration 3: Listing memories → mapping myself

**Question:** Where in my own life is the raw material for my story?

**Hypothesis:** If "the content comes from my life" is true, then my real memories should already contain the tension I care about: wanting a meaningful life, but not being able to reach it or say what it is.

**What I did:** I gave myself one rule: write down ten real moments from my life, without trying to make them good. I wrote nine (notebook pages 71–78):

1. **Always interrogating myself.** Do I really like games, reading, writing? I give an answer and cross it out. 100% or nothing, and in the end I deny everything about myself.
2. **A night during application season.** My topic was rejected again. I cried and wrote for an hour, cursing myself, took anxiety and depression self-tests, and at 4–5 a.m. sat against the wardrobe looking for doctors. Once I believed I was probably ill, I became calm. My dad found me a little after six, and I asked my parents to take me to the hospital.
3. **I thought I wanted change and freedom.** But my fear of the unknown is huge. I actually liked my days in high school.
4. **I thought my life was like the boy in "Lamenting Zhongyong"**, a prodigy who grew up ordinary. I believed mediocrity was a sin and wanted to be remembered.
5. **I'm drawn to certain titles**: *The Naive and the Sentimental Novelist*, *The World as Will and Representation*, *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*, *Klingsor's Last Summer*, the brain in a vat. I don't know why.
6. **My life went downhill after the gaokao**, because of architecture and urban planning. The work felt bureaucratic and absurd, and I felt I'd lost both my dream and my bread.
7. **Before college I was the one in color in a black-and-white photo.** Since college everything is in a fog, and I've faded too.
8. **In high school I read three books a week**, hiding from my parents like a spy. My memory was clear. Now everything is as if I've become nearsighted.
9. **As a child I hated my mom and fought her.** Now it's mostly guilt. I feel I failed her investment, but I don't know what she wants, and I feel she will never be satisfied.

**What I found:**

- **Almost every entry has the same shape:** *"I always thought I was X; it turned out I was Y."* The story isn't one event. It's a self-image breaking again and again.
- **There's a line from clear to foggy.** High school is "in color" and clear; after college it's fog, faded, nearsighted.
- **The inner voices were already there.** I didn't have to invent them: *Mom* ("I failed her investment"), *the fallen prodigy* ("mediocrity is a sin"), and *the interrogator* ("Are my thoughts even real?"). They are cold. They don't argue; they pass judgment.
- **There's already an arc:** wanting an absolute answer → getting one (a hospital, medicine) → it still doesn't fix anything ("seems controllable, but I still break down").
- **The contradiction underneath everything:** *wanting the absolute* ⇄ *fading into fog*. This might be the engine of the story.

**Reflection:** Doing this was harder than I expected, and more useful. When I looked at the map, the "This Game / Story" box was still empty, and next to it I had written "What do I want to say?" and, in green, "philosophy?" I still don't know the story. But for the first time, the material feels like mine and not borrowed.

## Exploration 4: Taking apart the opening of Disco Elysium

**Question:** I keep saying I want Disco Elysium's "temperament." What is it actually made of?

**What I did:** I replayed the opening: the conversation in the dark, waking up in the room, and the mirror. I interacted with everything in the room, screenshotted almost every line, and wrote down what I felt on a sticky note.

![[DE开头-便利贴.jpg|240]]

*Hangover · voices in my head → different voice actors · the brain fighting over resources → sets the tone · guitar swelling → waking up · crawling up naked → rebirth · the ceiling fan → a deliberately drunk walk*

Two more things stayed with me:

- Most of the voices in his head feel **cold, like some ancient creature talking**.
- The moment I remember most is the **mirror**. You see your miserable, hungover face, and one option is to keep insisting you're a *superstar*. With that swollen face that looks like a bitter smile, it's absurd.

**What I learned** (each as a principle, not a scene to copy):

1. **Start after the lowest point.** The game doesn't show how he destroyed himself; it starts after. He has lost his memory, and you rebuild who he is from objects and hints around you. "Rebuilding yourself" becomes the structure. Crawling up naked makes waking up feel like being born.
2. **Split one person into many voices.** Each inner voice has its own voice actor, and they fight for control of the body. "I" am not one whole person but an internal struggle, and that sets the tone of the whole game. *Every voice needs its own recognizable sound and way of speaking.*
3. **The temperature of the voices is the temperature of the work.** The voices in the dark are named after the oldest parts of the brain (the Ancient Reptilian Brain, the Limbic System). That's why they feel ancient: they make the inner world feel huge, strange, a little frightening. The voices in my own head are like that too: a cold, merciless judge that only questions and denies, that shows up when I'm anxious or lost, and never when I'm happy.
4. **Use sound to tell the story.** The move from darkness to waking up happens through music, not images: the guitar grows and he wakes. The sea wind and the ceiling fan do the same.
5. **The body comes before the mind.** His condition shows in how you move and interact: the stagger, the reactions to things in the room. You *operate* a wrecked body instead of just reading about it, and there's something funny in it. *For me, "brain fog, nearsighted, faded" could become something you see or feel in the controls.*
6. **Let the player choose to believe the self-myth.** The mirror doesn't judge you; it lets you decide whether to keep believing you're a superstar. The absurdity doesn't come from a joke. It comes from the gap between self-image and reality.
7. **A room is a self-portrait.** (From my own behavior: I couldn't stop clicking everything.) Every object is a clue to who he was. Searching it feels like going through your own drawers.

**Comparing it with my own material** (to compare, not to copy):

| Opening of Disco Elysium | What matches it in my material |
|---|---|
| Waking up hungover, the morning after collapse | Breaking down at 4–5 a.m.; my dad opening the door after six |
| Several voices fighting for control | Mom, the fallen prodigy, the interrogator |
| Cold, ancient voices | Sentences like verdicts: "mediocrity is a sin," "she will never be satisfied" |
| The superstar gap in the mirror | "The one in color in a black-and-white photo," who later faded |
| The room as a self-portrait | ? (not yet) |

**A difference I feel so far** (a hypothesis to test): Disco Elysium is loud, talkative, outwardly absurd, and political. My material is quieter: fog, fading, nearsightedness, guilt, and "once I believed I was ill, I became calm."

**Reflection:** After playing, I tried to write my own scene right away, and it immediately turned into a copy of the mirror scene with a different prop. So I'm separating the two: this exploration was for learning *how* it works, and my own scene has to come from my own question.

## What changed

- Before, I had a mood and a list of references. Now I have **raw material that's mine** (the memories and the map), **a core contradiction** (wanting the absolute vs. fading into fog), and **a set of principles** I learned from Disco Elysium, instead of a scene I want to copy.
- What I still don't have is the story: what I want to say, and where it starts.
